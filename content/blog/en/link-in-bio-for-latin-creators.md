If half your comments come in English and the other half in Spanish, your bio link has to work for both groups, and that is most of what a link in bio for Latin creators needs to get right. On TitiLinks, "bilingual" means the page's own buttons, labels and system text appear in English or Spanish to match each visitor's browser. Your link titles and bio are never translated for you: you write them, in both languages or in Spanglish.

## Why a split audience breaks a normal link-in-bio page

Most link-in-bio pages are written in one language, usually the one the creator thinks in, with titles like "New video" and "Shop my merch." That holds up until your Spanish-speaking followers from TikTok land on a page that only speaks English, or your English-speaking followers from Instagram hit a wall of Spanish. They can't tell at a glance which link is for them, and people rarely stay long on a bio link.

The usual fixes have costs of their own. Writing every link twice doubles the page and pushes the link you care about below the fold. Picking one language keeps the page short, but the other half of your audience stops clicking. A second account solves the language problem and creates a new one: two pages to keep current and two sets of numbers to compare.

## What "bilingual" means on TitiLinks

Your page carries two kinds of text. The first is what TitiLinks puts there: buttons, labels, form placeholders and the "Made with TitiLinks" credit. That text follows the visitor's browser language, the same way TitiLinks' own signup pages do. A fan whose phone is set to Spanish sees the default ticket button on your events as "Comprar entradas" and a sold-out show marked "Agotado"; a fan set to English sees "Get tickets" and "Sold out." The second kind is everything you write: link titles and your bio. TitiLinks shows those exactly as you typed them, with no translation. The interface adapts by itself, and the words on your links come from you, in English, in Spanish or in the mix your audience already speaks. The editor runs in English or Spanish too, whichever you prefer.

## Five tactics for a link in bio for Latin creators

1. Write link titles both ways, or in natural Spanglish. "Nuevo video / New video" is longer than one language but still reads at a glance if you keep both halves short, and fans of either language spot their link. Where your audience already code-switches, write the way you talk in your videos; "Merch nuevo" can be enough.
2. Order links for the platform your traffic comes from. If most visitors arrive from TikTok, put what TikTok viewers asked for at the top, in the language you use there. If Instagram sends more, lead with that audience. A page can't reorder itself for each visitor, so pick the order that fits your bigger source and revisit it when that changes.
3. Keep one page, not two accounts. One URL in every bio is easier for fans to remember and easier for you to keep current, and all your clicks land in one place. If you do want separate English and Spanish pages, Pro adds a second page to the same account, opened by adding ?page=2 to your URL.
4. Name buttons by outcome. "Mi curso / My course" tells people what they get, and "Click here" doesn't, in either language. Start each title with the thing or the action, as in "Escúchalo en Spotify / Listen on Spotify" or "Escríbeme / Message me."
5. Keep the top three links stable. Regular visitors learn where things are. If your newest release, your store and your WhatsApp always sit in the first three spots, repeat fans tap without reading, whatever language they speak. Rotate what sits below them instead.

## Example: a layout for a bilingual musician

Say you're a singer in Los Angeles who posts in Spanish on TikTok and in English on Instagram. A page that works for both could look like this:

- A clear photo, your name and a one-line bio in the mix you use on camera, such as "Cantante de LA. New single out now."
- Social icons for TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Spotify.
- "Nuevo sencillo / New single," linking to the song on Spotify or YouTube.
- "Fechas del tour / Tour dates," an events block with a ticket link for each date. The default ticket button label follows each visitor's language, and past dates drop off the page by themselves.
- "Merch," product cards with a photo and price, each linking to your store.
- "Videos," a YouTube feed block, so new uploads appear without editing the page.
- "Escríbeme / Message me," a WhatsApp link with a prefilled message, set up with the WhatsApp link builder.
- "Contrataciones / Booking," linking to your booking email.

## What to measure in your first month

Give the page a month before you change much. What you can see depends on your plan:

| Visits and total clicks, last 7 and 30 days | Analytics | Free |
| Clicks on each short link you create | Short links | Free (3 links) |
| Which sites your clicks go to | Analytics, top destinations | Pro |
| Where visitors come from: TikTok, Instagram or other | Analytics, traffic sources | Pro |

On the free plan, short links give you a rough split by platform. Make one short link for your TikTok bio and one for your Instagram bio, point both at your page, and compare their clicks after a few weeks. On Pro, the traffic sources card shows that split directly.

Then make one change: if one platform clearly sends more visitors, rewrite your top link in the language that audience uses, and leave the rest alone for another month so you can tell whether it worked.
